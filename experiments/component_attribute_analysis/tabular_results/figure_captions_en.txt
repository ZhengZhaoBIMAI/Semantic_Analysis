Figure. Tabular structure of observed BIM object attributes in the Stage1 audit snapshot (2026-10-14; 215 models and 93,359 graph objects). (A) Non-empty cell percentage after expanding the observed attribute union into fixed columns. The first row uses the global attribute union; each class-specific row uses the union observed in that class. Occupancy is the number of observed non-empty key–value occurrences divided by the number of objects multiplied by the corresponding union size. Structural blanks include inapplicable and unprovided attributes and do not measure semantic incompleteness. (B) Object-level distributions of non-empty attribute counts. Boxes span the first and third quartiles, the central line denotes the median, and whiskers extend to the most extreme observed values within 1.5 interquartile ranges from the box. Dots show observations beyond the whiskers; overlapping points may coincide. The horizontal axis is logarithmic (base 2), and all observations remain represented. (C) Distribution of the number of models supporting each object-class–attribute pair, pooled across the seven object classes. Each pair is counted once, and support requires at least one object with a non-empty value in a model; the eligible model count varies by class. Counts and percentages are shown above the bars. These panels describe observed full property paths before comprehensive synonym or schema harmonization, not a final cleaned WP1 table, semantic quality, or downstream model performance. Zero and false are non-empty values. The results provide motivation for flexible attribute representations, not comparative validation of a tokenization method.

Sensitivity figure. The same definitions, axes and boxplot conventions are used after excluding model_6. The retained 214 models contain 92,338 objects. Attribute unions and model support are recomputed from retained models; columns found only in model_6 are removed. Consequently, this is a refitted-vocabulary sensitivity analysis, not deletion of rows under a frozen global schema. CSV and JSON also report occupancy under the frozen baseline vocabulary for comparison.
